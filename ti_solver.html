Gas-solid equilibrium · Ti–O

Can this gas reduce this titanium oxide?

Gibbs energy minimisation with condensed phases handled as an active set: a phase that is absent is held at exactly zero and carries a KKT reduced cost saying how far it is from appearing. Free initial gas composition over CO2, H2, CO, H2O, N2, O2. Everything runs in this file — no external requests, no server.

Scope. Species-limited C–H–O–N / Ti–O Gibbs equilibrium. Gas registry: CO2, H2, CO, H2O, N2, O2. Excluded: CH4, graphite, nitrides, and the rutile TiO2−x solution phase. This is a closed, finite-inventory equilibrium contact between one gas charge and one solid charge — not a flow-reactor steady state. Every reported number is gibbs_min.

Method

The problem is the minimum of the total Gibbs energy

G = Σgas ni [μ°i(T) + RT ln(yiP/P°)] + Σsolid mj μ°j(T)

subject to the elemental balance A·n = b and n ≥ 0. Gases are ideal and mix; each condensed phase is a pure line compound at unit activity. The inputs that define b are the initial gas composition, the gas charge ng,0 = PV/RTcharge, and the initial solid phase and mass — the equilibrium phase split is never an input.

Condensed phases are an active set. For each candidate subset of phases (every single and every pair — Gibbs' phase rule allows two generic line compounds in a two-component M–O solid system at fixed T, P; N2 adds no solid axis), the equilibrium conditions become

gas:  μi = Σe aieλe    active solids:  μ°j = tjλTi + ojλO

solved by Newton in the element potentials λ with the gas in logarithmic coordinates; the solid amounts then come out of the Ti and O balances linearly, through the combination O − ρhost·Ti that removes the host oxygen symbolically. A phase at 10−41 mol costs the solver nothing, and a phase not in the candidate is exactly zero because it is not a variable at all.

The decision is KKT. Every inactive phase gets a reduced cost rj = μ°j − tjλTi − ojλO, reported per formula unit and per mole of oxygen (they differ by defj = 2tj−oj, which is 1 for every TiO2→Magnéli step and not 1 in general). The program is convex, so a candidate that is feasible with all rj ≥ 0 is the global minimum — candidates are never ranked by comparing total G values that double precision cannot resolve. Ties within tolerance are reported as degenerate boundaries, not silently resolved.

Why a bone-dry feed always sits on a buffer. Under pure H2 or pure N2 the candidate “rutile alone” leaves the gas with zero oxygen inventory, and the derivative of G toward creating a trace of O-bearing gas plus a reduced phase is −∞ (the ξ·lnξ edge). So the solver reports the two-phase buffer with a genuinely tiny extent — under sealed N2 that trace, at 10−41 mol, is the answer — while 10 ppm of O2 in the same nitrogen shuts reduction off entirely, with a strictly positive margin.

The oxygen-potential route that used to be called an independent cross-check is the dual of this same problem: its “margin” equals the KKT reduced cost per mole of oxygen. It lives on as exactly that, on the Active set & KKT tab. Independence comes from the 80-digit oracle and the NIST-vs-Waldner data comparison on the Validation tab.
