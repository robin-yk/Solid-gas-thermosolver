Defect-state distribution

Calculates the equilibrium distribution of a measured oxygen-vacancy inventory across rutile sites.

Model scope and assumptions
Scope. C–H–O–N / Ti–O Gibbs equilibrium. Gas registry in this version: CO2, H2, CO, H2O, N2, O2. Hydrocarbon species (CH4), graphite, nitrides, and the rutile TiO2−x solution phase are not included in this version. The calculation is a closed, finite-inventory equilibrium between one gas charge and one solid charge, not a flow-reactor steady state. Every reported value is gibbs_min.

Method

The solver computes the minimum of the total Gibbs energy

G = Σgas ni [μ°i(T) + RT ln(yiP/P°)] + Σsolid mj μ°j(T)

subject to the elemental balance A·n = b and n ≥ 0. Gases form an ideal mixture; each condensed phase is a pure line compound at unit activity. The inputs defining b are the initial gas composition, the gas charge ng,0 = PV/RTcharge, and the initial solid phase and mass. The equilibrium phase split is an output.

Active set. Condensed phases are enumerated as candidates: every single phase and every phase pair (the phase rule allows two line compounds in an M–O system at fixed T and P; N2 adds no solid component). For each candidate the equilibrium conditions are

gas:  μi = Σe aieλe    active solids:  μ°j = tjλTi + ojλO

solved by Newton iteration in the element potentials λ with the gas in logarithmic coordinates. Condensed amounts follow linearly from the Ti and O balances through the combination O − ρhost·Ti, which removes the host oxygen symbolically; coexisting phases are resolved down to ~10−41 mol. A phase outside the candidate is not a variable and is exactly zero in the result.

Stability criterion. Each inactive phase carries a reduced cost rj = μ°j − tjλTi − ojλO, reported per formula unit and per mole of oxygen (defj = 2tj − oj; defj = 1 for each TiO2→Magnéli step). The problem is convex, so a feasible candidate with all rj ≥ 0 is the global minimum. Candidates are not ranked by total Gibbs energy, whose differences can fall below double-precision resolution. Reduced costs at zero within tolerance are reported as phase coexistence.

Oxygen-free feeds. Under pure H2 or N2 the single-phase candidate leaves the gas with zero oxygen inventory, and the derivative of G toward forming a trace of O-bearing gas together with a reduced phase diverges. The solver therefore reports the two-phase buffer with a finite trace amount; under sealed N2 this trace is the equilibrium result. An O2 impurity at the ppm level is sufficient to suppress reduction, with a positive margin.

The oxygen-potential margin used by earlier versions of this site equals the reduced cost per mole of oxygen (the dual of the same minimisation); it is reported on the Active set & KKT tab. Independent verification is provided by an 80-digit reference implementation and by the NIST–Waldner data comparison (Validation tab).

Model scope and assumptions


  Scope. Canonical lattice statistical mechanics for a measured oxygen-vacancy
  inventory in rutile TiO2: the total VO is fixed from experiment
  and its conditional-equilibrium partition over (110) bridging-O, first-subsurface and
  bulk sites is computed at equal vacancy chemical potential — the thermodynamic
  tendency for redistribution of a prescribed inventory, not reduction kinetics.
  Site energies are polaron-relaxed literature DFT values; Ti3+ polarons are
  not explicit variables in this version. The reported 17–20% onset of the
  (1×2) reconstruction is treated as a competing surface phase, not a cap:
  at the onset the (1×1) vacancy lattice gas coexists with the added-row
  Ti2O3 line phase (lever rule at pinned μV,
  areal O deficiency 0.5 bridging-ML), and beyond the bulk point-defect solubility
  μV pins at the estimated rutile/CS coexistence with the excess reported
  as extended defects. Nothing is clipped and no remainder is silently reassigned;
  the idealisations of each phase branch are declared in data/rutile_dft.json.

Vacancy distribution

      
Conditional-equilibrium partition of the measured inventory at the
      common vacancy chemical potential, using the geometric site counts of the
      spherical particle and the phase-aware surface (the (1×2) reconstruction
      and the CS ceiling enter as phases). The partition uses the formation energies
      alone; the sampled surface Monte Carlo feeds only the ordering statistics below,
      because its pair terms are effective parameters constrained to arrangement
      statistics, not layer energetics.

Reoxidation kinetics — CGMC

      
Coarse-grained kinetic Monte Carlo of the whole particle during
      one CO2 exposure: radial coarse cells, Arrhenius exchange rates in
      detailed balance with the (1×1) site-class Hamiltonian, and the
      irreversible surface refill as the only gas channel. With the neutral-vacancy
      barrier, transport is not rate-limiting at 600 °C — a measured partial
      recovery therefore reads back as an effective migration barrier.

      

      

        
      

      

      

        
      

      

      

    


    

      Model formulation and references
      

      
We calculate the conditional-equilibrium partitioning of the measured vacancy
      inventory to determine the thermodynamic tendency for vacancy redistribution
      among surface, subsurface and bulk environments. Three site classes hold the
      fixed total: rutile (110) bridging O, the first subsurface O layer (the in-plane
      and sub-bridging O of the outer trilayer — removal of non-bridging oxygen
      is this class), and bulk. The partition equalises the vacancy chemical potential
      using the analytic site-exclusion isotherms on the formation energies alone.
      Surface lateral ordering is a separate calculation: effective pair interactions,
      constrained to reproduce the vacancy-correlation statistics reported by
      Birschitzky et al., are sampled by canonical Metropolis Monte Carlo with Widom
      insertion, and inform the arrangement exhibits only — they never enter the
      layer partition.

      
Shell thickness is a declared model choice, not a fitted quantity. The
      (1×1) cell carries one bridging O, while every d110 = 0.325 nm
      oxygen slab below the bridging plane carries four O per cell, so one subsurface
      layer is four bridging rows of capacity (54.2 against 13.6 μmol-O/g at
      0.9 μm) and the class is never a single atomic row. The default of one layer
      follows the DFT set, which resolves one first-subsurface energy and takes deeper
      layers as bulk. Because that energy lies far below the bulk value, the shell/bulk
      split moves with the choice: at 600 °C and 95 μmol-O/g the subsurface class
      holds 51.8 / 82.6 / 86.3 / 87.1 μmol-O/g for one to four layers, the bulk takes
      36.5 / 5.6 / 2.0 / 1.2, and μV falls from 0.819 to 0.560 eV. Real
      formation energies relax smoothly towards the bulk value over the first few
      layers, so the physical answer lies between the one- and two-layer columns. What
      moves is the size of the bulk remainder, not the conclusion that the near-surface
      shell carries most of the inventory (62% of it at one layer, 94% at two). The
      ladder is certified against the 50-digit oracle and the control in the input
      panel reproduces it.

      
Two phase branches close the construction. The (1×2) reconstruction
      enters as a first-order surface transition at the reported onset: the (1×1)
      lattice gas coexists with the added-row Ti2O3 line phase
      (Onishi–Iwasawa stoichiometry, one O vacancy per two bridging sites,
      θeff = 0.5; confirmed stable under reduction by Reticcioli et
      al., Phys. Rev. X 7, 031053 (2017)), with the lever rule at pinned
      μV. The high-coverage branch of the ideal (1×1) gas is
      excluded from the phase set on physical grounds (repulsive ordering energies;
      the observed strongly reduced surface is reconstructed, not bridging-stripped).
      Beyond the bulk point-defect solubility (xsol ≈ 0.008 in
      TiO2−x, an estimate), μV pins at the rutile/CS
      coexistence and the excess precipitates as extended defects. The particle-scale
      balance uses the real geometric site counts: at 0.9 μm the bridging-O layer
      holds at most 13.6 μmol-O/g (0.054% of all O sites), so a 95 μmol-O/g
      inventory drives the surface through the reconstruction (complete by
      ∼6.8 μmol-O/g) and loads the subsurface shell to ∼95% occupancy,
      just below the estimated CS ceiling (∼160 μmol-O/g at 600 °C).
      The certified boundary ladder is printed above the results; the measured BET
      area (1.60 m²/g) and the diameter model agree on it to ∼2%.

      
Site energies are polaron-relaxed DFT values: the oxygen vacancy itself is most
      stable in the surface layer, while the Ti3+ small polarons it donates
      prefer subsurface Ti (Reticcioli et al., Phys. Rev. B 98, 045306 (2018)). The
      single subsurface energy is applied to all four O sites of the first layer
      (a data-granularity limit; per-site project DFT will split the class in the
      dataset). Explicit polaron variables and the grand-canonical coupling to the
      gas-phase oxygen potential are later stages of this workspace. The kinetic
      transport layer implements the group's coarse-grained kinetic Monte Carlo
      framework (Katsoulakis & Vlachos, J. Chem. Phys. 119, 9412 (2003), DOI
      10.1063/1.1616513; Katsoulakis, Majda & Vlachos, Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA
      100, 782 (2003)): detailed-balance exchange rates on radial coarse cells,
      reducing in the dilute limit to spherical diffusion, with the CO2
      surface refill as the one irreversible channel.

      
Dataset. Layer energies: Li, Guo & Robertson, J. Phys. Chem. C 119
      (2015), DOI 10.1021/acs.jpcc.5b02430: sX 0 / +0.59 / +1.31 eV, GGA
      0 / +0.39 / +1.08 eV (surface / first subsurface / bulk); the ordering survives the
      change of functional. Surface ordering constraints: Birschitzky et al., npj Comput.
      Mater. 10 (2024), DOI 10.1038/s41524-024-01289-4: in-row spacings of 1–2
      bridging sites nearly absent, modal spacing 4–5 sites, experimental average
      reconstruction onset ∼17%, DFT transition ∼20%, locally rich regions up
      to ∼20%, and interaction range ∼10 Å. These percentages define a
      reconstruction interval and do not define a maximum vacancy capacity.
      The pair energies here are calibrated to those constraints. Project DFT
      configuration energies will replace them directly when those values
      arrive. Every model parameter lives in data/rutile_dft.json. Dielectric
      correlation: measured in this work. The power-law fit and its statistics are
      shown on the accessibility panel. Diffusion barriers stored for kinetics:
      Iddir et al., J. Phys. Chem. C (2010); Wang et al., Phys. Rev. B 82, 165309
      (2010).
