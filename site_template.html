Why it stops at Ti4O7

Each reduction step needs the gas to fall below a threshold H2O/H2 ratio. The steps get harder as the oxide gets more reduced, and the gas an actual feed settles at sits well above the second rung — so the solid parks on the TiO2/Ti4O7 two-phase buffer and stays there.

Is Ti3O5 even a real phase here?

A phase that sits between two others is only stable if it does not disproportionate into them. For Ti4O7 + Ti2O3 ↔ 2 Ti3O5, negative ΔG means Ti3O5 holds together.

Phases in the calculation

Where this is soft

  • The Magneli series is truncated. Ti5O9 through Ti10O19 sit between rutile and Ti4O7 and are absent. They form more easily than Ti4O7, so they are the phases that could still overturn the RWGS null result at the high-temperature end.
  • Condensed phases are pure and immiscible. Real Magneli formation runs through a homologous series with mutual solubility, so the true onset is gradual where this model puts a sharp branch.
  • No carbon deposition. Graphite is not a species, so the CH4 results assume all carbon leaves as CO. Adding C(graphite) opens Boudouard and cracking equilibria that could shift them.
  • Equilibrium only. Nothing here says how fast any of it happens, or whether a real surface passivates first.
