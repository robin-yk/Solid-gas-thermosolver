Pick a gas and conditions; the page equilibrates the C–H–O gas, reads off its oxygen potential, and compares that against every titanium oxide in the series. Everything is computed in the browser from NIST-JANAF and Waldner–Eriksson coefficients — nothing here is a stored answer.

What this does and does not model

  • Line compounds only. Each oxide is present or absent at unit activity. Real reduction runs through a homologous series with mutual solubility, and at the shallow end through oxygen-deficient rutile TiO2-x, which no set of line compounds can represent. That is why the answer keeps moving as you deepen the series — try the depth selector.
  • No carbon deposition. Graphite is not a species, so under CH4 all carbon leaves as CO. The gas comes out at H2:CO = 2:1 exactly, which is what happens when carbon has nowhere else to go. Treat the CH4 numbers as provisional.
  • Sealed vessel. The inert case assumes a closed headspace. Flowing gas sweeps the released O2 away and is an open-system question this does not answer.
  • Equilibrium only. Nothing here says how fast any of it happens.
Source and the Python package: robin-yk/Solid-gas-thermosolver. Data: NIST-JANAF 4th ed. (Chase, 1998) for gases; Waldner & Eriksson, Calphad 23 (1999) 189–218 for Ti–O condensed phases.
